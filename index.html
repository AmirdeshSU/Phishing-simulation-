What to do differently next time

  • 01
    Check the sender's actual address Not just the display name. Hover or tap to reveal the real email address — look for misspelled domains or extra characters (e.g. "micros0ft.com").
  • 02
    Pause on urgency and pressure "Act now," "your account will be suspended," "verify immediately" are designed to make you skip the checks below.
  • 03
    Hover before you click Preview the actual destination URL. If it doesn't match the company or service it claims to be from, don't click.
  • 04
    Never enter credentials from an email link Go directly to the site by typing the URL yourself or using a saved bookmark, instead of logging in through a link.
  • 05
    Watch for generic or slightly-off greetings "Dear user," odd phrasing, or formatting that doesn't match the real company's usual emails are red flags.
  • 06
    Be wary of unexpected attachments Especially .zip, .exe, or macro-enabled Office files you weren't expecting — even from familiar-looking senders.
  • 07
    Report it, don't just delete it Use your organization's "Report Phishing" button or forward it to your IT/security team so others can be warned.
